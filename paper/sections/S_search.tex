\section{Search strategy}
\label{app:search}

This section reproduces the search of \S4 in full, one source at a time, so that it can be re-run
without the replication package. All sources were searched on a single day, 2026-09-16: the database
harvest is logged from 19:47 to 20:50 UTC, the two survey bibliographies were snowballed from 20:53
to 20:58 UTC, and the search was frozen at 20:59 UTC, when the last deduplication wrote the candidate
set. The information sources are arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, the ACL Anthology, OpenReview and
GitHub, supplemented by citation searching (Semantic Scholar citations and references of eleven seed
papers, the bibliographies of two surveys that Semantic Scholar does not index, and three curated
``awesome'' lists) and by grey literature (vendor documentation and eight agent leaderboards). Every
database query is a conjunction of two blocks, a harness-vocabulary block and a language-model block
(Section~\ref{subsec:search-blocks}). The harness block is the second version, adopted under
amendment~1 after the registered known-item rule failed on the first; under amendment~2 the
language-model block is waived when one of seven strong harness terms is present, which applies on
arXiv, the ACL Anthology and OpenReview and not on the other three sources. On Semantic Scholar and
OpenAlex a third, structure block is added for precision. Every dated source is limited to the
release window 2022-10-01 to 2026-08-31 inclusive, by publication, submission or repository-creation
date as the source allows. No language filter is applied at search: the English-language requirement
is an eligibility criterion (protocol section 4.6), applied at screening. No source was capped
(\texttt{--max-records 0}). Table~\ref{tab:search-sources} gives the records each source returned;
the 50,724 raw records deduplicate to 27,747 candidates (22,977 duplicates removed), matching on DOI,
then arXiv identifier, then exact normalized title, then a fuzzy title match at ratio 95 or above.

\subsection{Search blocks}
\label{subsec:search-blocks}

The blocks as registered, with their amendments (protocol section 6). An asterisk is a
right-truncation wildcard; quotation marks enclose a phrase. In the listings, lines are wrapped for
print only at spaces between tokens, never inside a quoted phrase, and an indented line continues the
one above it.

\noindent\textbf{Harness block (v2, amendment 1).}
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
"agent harness" OR harness OR "agent scaffold*" OR "agentic framework" OR
  "agent framework" OR "LLM agent*" OR "LM agent*" OR "language agent*" OR "AI agent*"
  OR "computer agent*" OR "multi-agent" OR "tool-use agent" OR "coding agent" OR
  "software engineering agent" OR "computer-use agent" OR "GUI agent" OR "web agent" OR
  "multi-agent framework" OR "agent orchestration" OR "LLM orchestration"
\end{verbatim}
}

\noindent\textbf{Language-model block.}
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
"large language model" OR LLM OR "foundation model" OR "language model agent"
\end{verbatim}
}

\noindent\textbf{Strong harness terms that waive the language-model block (amendment 2).} Where the
waiver applies, a record is retrieved if it matches (harness block AND language-model block) OR any
of:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
"agent harness*" OR "agentic harness*" OR "coding agent*" OR "agent scaffold*" OR
  "agentic scaffold*" OR "software engineering agent*" OR "web agent*"
\end{verbatim}
}

\noindent\textbf{Structure block (Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex only).}
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
"tool call*" OR "function call*" OR "control loop" OR "context management" OR memory OR
  sandbox OR verification OR retry OR planning
\end{verbatim}
}

\noindent\textbf{ACL Anthology and OpenReview.} Neither offers a Boolean query interface, so every
record in the window is fetched and filtered locally: a case-insensitive regular expression built
from each block (\texttt{common.HARNESS\_RE} and \texttt{common.LLM\_RE} in
\texttt{scripts/harvest/common.py}) is applied to title and abstract, keeping a record that matches
(harness AND language-model) OR a strong term. The expression lets a hyphen or space inside a phrase
match either (\texttt{"tool-use agent"} matches ``tool use agent''), makes an asterisk a word-character
wildcard, and tolerates a trailing plural \emph{s} on the last word of every term.

\noindent\textbf{GitHub} (\texttt{gh api search/repositories}, nine queries). A repository returned by
any of them is kept when its README or description matches the language-model block regex; the
strong-term waiver is not applied, because the full READMEs of the rejected repositories were not
cached.

\subsection{Citation searching and grey literature}
\label{subsec:search-other}

\emph{Snowballing.} One round of forward and backward citation searching was run through the Semantic
Scholar citations and references endpoints from eleven seeds: the two competitor surveys that
Semantic Scholar indexes (arXiv 2606.20683 and 2604.03515), six further harness papers (2605.12239,
2609.00006, 2605.13357, 2605.27922, 2607.22585, 2608.26218), and SWE-agent (2405.15793), OpenHands
(2407.16741) and ReAct (2210.03629). Semantic Scholar listed 13,269 citing and cited records, of which
11,289 fall in the window and were kept (11,024 citations and 265 references). ReAct's citations stop
at 9,999, the most the API returns for one paper, so its citing set is truncated.

\emph{Survey bibliographies.} The other two competitor surveys have no Semantic Scholar record: Li et
al., \emph{Agent Harness Engineering: A Survey} (OpenReview eONq7FdiHa), and Meng et al., \emph{Agent
Harness for Large Language Model Agents: A Survey} (Preprints.org 202604.0428). Their reference lists
were parsed from the PDFs (250 and 170 entries) and each entry resolved on Semantic Scholar by arXiv
identifier or DOI, else by title at a fuzzy-match ratio of 85 or above. Of the 420 entries, 5 fall
outside the window and 415 were kept: 195 resolved papers, 81 repositories and 139 blog or
documentation references.

\emph{Curated lists.} Three curated catalogues, two of them companions of the competitor surveys,
were harvested in full, without date or block filters: \texttt{Picrew/awesome-agent-harness} (368
entries), \texttt{Gloriaameng/Awesome-Agent-Harness} (158) and \texttt{ggjy/Awesome-Agent-Engineering}
(296), 822 records.

\emph{Vendor documentation.} Official documentation pages were fetched for 48 closed or vendor
systems (from Claude Code, Codex CLI and Gemini CLI to AutoGen and OpenHands); of 185 URLs tried, 160
resolved and 25 failed, and every system has at least one resolving page, so each contributes one
record.

\emph{Leaderboards.} SWE-bench, HAL, OSWorld, WebArena (with VisualWebArena), Terminal-Bench, GAIA,
tau-bench and tau2-bench were harvested from their public data (4,893 entries, 3,946 records, one per
system per leaderboard), for the outcomes table and to find systems that have no paper. GAIA's test
split is an open submission log of mostly one-off names, so it is excluded from the candidate set;
the other leaderboards contribute 251 systems. The Terminal-Bench 2.0 leaderboard could not be
obtained.

\emph{Supplementary arm.} The independent search of amendment~7 (2026-09-20, an Elicit
systematic-review pipeline given the same definition and eligibility criteria) returned 987 records,
of which it included 411; 103 of those were not among the frozen candidates and enter the review
through PRISMA's ``other methods'' arm. Its five queries, semantic and keyword, were written by the
pipeline, and their strings are not recorded in the release.

\subsection{Validation against known items}
\label{subsec:search-validation}

The registered validation rule requires known-item recall of at least 90\%. On the test run, the
first harness block found 3 of 12 canonical harness papers on arXiv (4,030 records). The misses
described themselves as ``LM agents'', ``LLM agents'', ``AI agents'', ``computer agents'', a
``multi-agent'' framework, or a bare ``harness'', none of them a phrase of the block, and one, ReAct,
never uses the word ``agent'' in its abstract. A broadened block found 11 of 12 (11,834 records), ReAct
being left to snowballing. Amendment~1 adopted that block, replacing bare \emph{orchestration} with
``agent orchestration'' and ``LLM orchestration'' (the harness block above).

At freeze, recall was measured on 48 known items, the arXiv identifiers of every entry in the review's
must-cite bibliography (\texttt{data/raw/known\_items.txt}). With the second block alone, the six
search sources found 31 of 48, and 42 of 48 with snowballing and the curated lists. Eight of the
misses matched the harness block but not the language-model block: the 2026 harness literature often
writes ``model'' or ``agent'' without ``LLM'' or ``large language model''. Amendment~2 waives the
language-model block for strong harness terms. Its nine-term form, measured first, added 963 arXiv
records, of which 12 in a random sample of 40 were clearly relevant and 15 clearly irrelevant, the
noise concentrated in ``GUI agent'' and ``computer-use agent''; the seven-term form adopted drops those
two, keeps all eight recovered items, and added 593 arXiv, 30 ACL and 68 OpenReview records. With it,
the searches alone find 39 of 48 known items (81\%), and 46 of 48 (96\%) with snowballing, the survey
bibliographies and the curated lists, above the 90\% threshold. The survey bibliographies added no
known item. The two still missing are arXiv 2607.10113, a survey of agent skills that uses no harness
term, and 2609.17394, dated 2026-09-15, after the window closes.
