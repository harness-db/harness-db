Abstract

Multimodal harmful content detection is challenging in zero-shot settings because novel memes highlight two key limitations: 1) contextual void, where retrieved similar memes lack rationale-based grounding for evolving cultural context; and 2) static reasoning rigidity, where fixed reasoning templates struggle with subtle cross-modal incongruities. To address this problem, we propose RAMTA, a case-based reasoning framework for zero-shot harmful content detection and open-world multimodal moderation. RAMTA implements three CBR-aligned strategies: 1) retrieval, by fetching rationale-enriched precedents rather than raw similar memes; 2) adaptation, by redefining case reuse as tool-plan adaptation, using retrieved cases to select a tailored subset of reasoning tools rather than directly transferring labels; and 3) revision, by employing a Dialectical Consensus Adjudicator to cross-examine multi-view evidence and synthesize the final verdict. Experiments on three benchmarks show that RAMTA achieves strong zero-shot results, including a +3.67% accuracy gain over MIND on HarM and a +10.45% gain over Gemini-2.0-Flash (CoT) with the same backbone, suggesting that case-guided adaptation is an effective CBR paradigm for multimodal moderation, highlighting the value of CBR in LLM agentic systems.
