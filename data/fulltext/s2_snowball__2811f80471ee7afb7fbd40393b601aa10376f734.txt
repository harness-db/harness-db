Abstract
Non-Player Characters (NPCs) play a central role in interactive environments, where adaptive and context-
aware behavior is essential for engaging gameplay. Traditional rule-based and utility-driven approaches often
lack adaptability and contextually coherent behaviors, while Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Large Lan -
guage Models (LLMs) offer complementary strengths but exhibit distinct limitations: RL suffers from train -
ing inefficiency and limited generalization, whereas LLMs are prone to hallucinations and context drift. This
paper introduces HeRoN, a mediated framework that integrates RL and LLMs through functional separation
and critique-based refinement to enable coherent and strategically adaptive NPC behavior. The architecture
comprises an RL-controlled NPC policy for action execution, an LLM-based strategy generator providing
context-aware action proposals, and a lightweight reviewer that refines these proposals to enforce consistency
with environment constraints. Through experiments in two structurally distinct custom game environments,
we show that early LLM-mediated guidance improves exploration efficiency and generalization. Compared to
standard RL baselines, HeRoN achieves up to an 81% improvement in task success rate while substantially
reducing constraint-violating actions.

1 Introduction
In video games, Non-Player Characters (NPCs) are in-game entities not directly controlled by players [ 1]. Far
from being passive elements, they shape player experience by influencing narrative depth, realism, and gameplay
dynamics [2]. Depending on their design, NPCs may act as opponents, allies, quest-givers, merchants, or ambient
characters, thereby influencing challenge, progression, and immersion [3, 4].
Early NPC design relied on scripted rules and finite state machines, later extended through Behavior Trees
(BTs) to manage hierarchical behaviors [ 5, 6]. While these approaches improved modularity, they remain dif -
ficult to scale in highly dynamic environments. Utility-based AI further enhanced adaptability through weighted
action evaluation [7], but required extensive manual tuning and often produced inconsistent or suboptimal behav-
ior. More recently, data-driven methods such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) [ 8] and Large Language Models
(LLMs) [9] have been explored. RL enables strategy optimization through interaction, whereas LLMs support
high-level, context-aware reasoning [10–12]. However, neither paradigm alone provides a complete solution [11,
13]: RL agents often exhibit slow convergence and limited generalization [14], while LLMs remain prone to hal-
lucinations and constraint violations [ 15, 16]. These limitations motivate mediated hybrid approaches in which
language-based reasoning is explicitly constrained and guided by environment-aware learning processes rather
than directly coupled to action execution [17].
Within this context, we introduce HeRoN, a mediated RL-LLM framework that integrates language guidance
into policy learning through functional separation rather than autonomous LLM control. The core idea is the
explicit decoupling between RL-based action selection and externalized language reasoning, whose influence is
mediated and constrained. This design mitigates common RL-LLM failure modes, including policy bias and hal-
lucinated or invalid actions, while remaining applicable across heterogeneous environments. HeRoN comprises
three components: (i) the NPC, an RL-controlled policy responsible for action execution; (ii) the Helper, an
LLM generating context-aware proposals; and (iii) the Reviewer, a lightweight critique module that refines
proposals to ensure consistency with environment constraints. Rather than operating as autonomous agents, these
components form a mediated decision pipeline in which the RL policy retains full control over action execution.
We evaluate the framework in a custom turn-based battle environment inspired by Japanese Role-Playing
Games (JRPGs), featuring discrete actions, constrained resources, delayed rewards, and a rich strategic space
[18]. Within this setting, we assess the contribution of the Reviewer in refining the Helper’s recommenda-
tions and analyze how different mediation schedules affect learning dynamics. Results show that the Reviewer
reduces hallucinated actions by up to 98% and improves action coherence, while structured early-stage guidance
yields testing gains of up to 67%. To assess robustness, we further validate HeRoN in a structurally distinct first-
person shooting environment (ViZDoom), reported in Appendix B. Across heterogeneous settings, early LLM-
mediated guidance enhances exploration efficiency and accelerates policy convergence, confirming that mediated
LLM assistance improves RL training without modifying reward structures or compromising policy stability.
The main contributions are: (i) introducing HeRoN, a mediated RL–LLM framework that formalizes language-
guided policy learning without altering reward functions; ( ii) proposing a critique-based mediation mechanism
that reduces hallucinations and enforces strategic coherence; and ( iii) empirically analyzing how the timing and
degree of mediation affect learning dynamics, highlighting the benefits of early guidance and the risks of exces -
sive late-stage intervention.

2 Related work
This section reviews the historical evolution of NPCs and examines recent RL-LLM integration approaches,
identifying key limitations that motivate HeRoN.
2.1 Intelligent NPCs in video games
The design of NPCs has evolved significantly over time, from simple scripted behaviors to structured decision-
making systems [ 19]. Early games such as Tennis for Two and Pac-Man relied on minimal AI logic [ 20, 21],
while the 1990 s introduced more strategic behavior in titles like Civilization and StarCraft [22]. In the 2000 s,
Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP), introduced by Jeff Orkin for F .E.A.R., enabled NPCs to autonomously
plan action sequences based on world state and objectives [ 23]. BTs later became a dominant paradigm in com-
mercial games such as Halo 2 and Halo 3 [24, 25], offering a hierarchical and modular structure that improved
design, reuse, and debugging compared to finite state machines [ 6]. These architectures further evolved into
Event-Driven BTs, where decisions are triggered dynamically by in-game events, enhancing responsiveness and
scalability [6].
Later, the integration of deep learning in the 2010 s enabled more realistic agents and large-scale procedural
generation, as exemplified by No Man’ s Sky [22, 26]. Advanced AI architectures also introduced hierarchical
designs, such as the two-tier system in The Last of Us , which separates high-level decision-making from low-
level execution to support modular and reusable behavior components [ 27, 28]. AAA titles have increasingly
adopted adaptive AI techniques to create NPCs capable of dynamically responding to player behavior [ 29].
Examples include The Last of Us Part II, where enemies coordinate and react contextually in real time, and the
Nemesis System in Middle-Earth: Shadow of Mordor, which procedurally evolves enemy traits and hierarchies
based on repeated player interactions [ 22, 30]. These systems illustrate a shift toward persistent, player-contin -
gent NPC adaptation in modern game design.
Since 2020, RL has begun to appear in commercial settings. Sony AI’s GT Sophy, deployed in Gran Turismo
7, remains the only documented case of an RL agent integrated into a commercially released title [ 31]. Ubisoft
La Forge has also explored RL for autonomous navigation in complex 3D environments, though generalization
beyond trained scenarios remains limited [32]. Despite their strategic capabilities, RL-based approaches still face
significant challenges in terms of training cost and environment scalability.
In parallel, LLMs have been investigated to enhance NPC interactivity through natural language understand -
ing and generation. Ubisoft’s NEO NPC prototype illustrates this direction, enabling open-ended dialogue via
LLMs [33]. Several indie titles, including Vaudeville, SuckUp, AI People, and Realms of Alterra, experiment with
LLM-driven characters that dynamically respond to player input [34–37]. However, these early implementations
highlight persistent issues such as hallucinations, memory inconsistencies, and incoherent or strategically mis -
aligned responses [34]. More broadly, most existing approaches employ LLMs as direct generators of dialogue
or actions, with limited mechanisms to mediate, critique, or constrain their influence on decision-making. As a
result, linguistically plausible outputs may violate game-specific constraints or long-term strategic objectives.
Despite recent progress, current RL- and LLM-based solutions have yet to achieve both computational effi -
ciency and broad deployability. RL methods often struggle with cross-environment generalization, while LLM-
driven NPCs risk ungrounded behavior without explicit control mechanisms. To address these limitations, we
introduce HeRoN, a lightweight mediated RL-LLM framework that integrates language-based guidance into

2.2 RL-LLM hybrid approaches
Recent research on RL-LLM integration has explored multiple paradigms, each exhibiting notable limitations
when applied to video games. Methods that employ LLMs for reward shaping [ 38, 39] rely on offline specifica-
tion and struggle to capture context-dependent strategic trade-offs that emerge dynamically during gameplay,
such as assessing the value of a healing item based on remaining enemy health and anticipated future resource
requirements. High-level planning approaches [ 40, 41] decompose tasks hierarchically but often suffer from
plan-execution misalignment in rapidly evolving environments and lack robust mechanisms for adaptive revi -
sion. Policy initialization techniques [ 42] leverage LLMs to provide useful behavioral priors. However, they
discontinue language-based guidance during the training phase, thereby offering no support when NPCs must
discover advanced mechanics, such as status effects or item synergies, through interaction. This limitation is
particularly critical in commercial deployment scenarios, where robust performance is required across multiple
difficulty levels. Learning-from-AI-feedback frameworks [43–45] typically operate at single-step granularity and
may produce unreliable critique signals, particularly in long-horizon strategic settings. Direct action-generation
approaches [46, 47] treat LLMs as policies but frequently yield invalid or ungrounded actions, while self-critique
methods [48, 49] face grounding challenges, especially in stochastic environments where strategic errors and
randomness are difficult to disentangle.
Beyond these architectural differences, prior paradigms rarely examine the temporal dimension of assis -
tance, specifically, when and for how long language-based guidance should be introduced during training. Most
approaches assume continuous or static integration of LLM support, overlooking how its timing may influence
exploration, stability, and policy consolidation.
HeRoN addresses both structural and temporal limitations through a unified framework that combines explicit
role mediation (Helper proposes, Reviewer validates, RL executes), selective timing of guidance, and cri -
tique-based validation enabled by a two-phase Reviewer training process. By jointly targeting hallucination
mitigation, game adherence, and phase-dependent intervention, the framework emphasizes that not only the pres-
ence of LLM guidance, but also the manner and timing of its integration, are critical for stable and deployment-
ready NPC behavior across diverse interactive scenarios.
3 The NPC decision-making problem
To design NPCs capable of context-aware and adaptive behavior across heterogeneous environments, we formal-
ize the decision-making problem independently of any specific game genre. The NPC control task is modeled as
a sequential decision process in which an agent interacts with a dynamic and potentially stochastic environment
that may include competitive or cooperative entities.
Although the formulation in this section focuses on a turn-based combat setting inspired by JRPGs, the frame-
work naturally extends to real-time and partially observable domains, including first-person shooters, as demon-
strated through experiments in ViZDoom (see Appendix B).

4 HeRoN framework
To address decision-making challenges in interactive environments, we introduce HeRoN (Helper-Reviewer-
NPC), a mediated RL-LLM framework that enhances NPC behavior through structured, critique-based language
assistance. The framework targets sequential decision problems in which an NPC must select context-appropriate
actions under uncertainty, delayed rewards, and partial observability, conditions common across turn-based and
real-time games.
The formalization introduced in the previous section highlights a class of problems where an NPC must bal -
ance short-term effectiveness with long-term strategic objectives, manage constrained resources, and adapt to
non-stationary opponent or environment dynamics. These characteristics are not tied to a specific game genre, but
instead arise broadly in interactive environments where decisions are sequential, consequences may be delayed,
and suboptimal choices can compound over time.
Unlike conventional multi-agent systems, HeRoN does not assume multiple autonomous agents with indepen-
dent objectives. Instead, learning and control are fully centralized within a single RL-driven NPC policy. LLMs
operate exclusively as auxiliary reasoning modules that influence decisions indirectly by generating and refining
action proposals. This separation also facilitates flexible deployment choices, as language-based components can
be replaced or reconfigured without modifying the underlying RL control loop.
As illustrated in Fig. 1, the framework comprises three functional components operating at distinct stages of
the decision pipeline:
● the NPC N, which directly interacts with the environment and executes actions;
● the Helper H, an LLM-based strategy generator that produces context-aware action proposals and accompa-
nying rationales;
● the Reviewer R, a lightweight LLM module that critiques and refines the Helper’s proposals to ensure
feasibility, rule compliance, and strategic coherence.
This architectural separation entails distinct learning roles. The NPC optimizes its policy through sequential inter-
action with the environment, while the Helper operates in a zero-shot setting, leveraging the inherent reasoning
capabilities of LLMs without task-specific training. The Reviewer addresses a single-step refinement task,
aligning the Helper’s proposals with environmental constraints and strategic coherence.

At each decision step t, the current environment state st is encoded into a structured natural-language prompt

5 Experimental design
In this section, we present the experimental evaluation of the proposed mediated RL-LLM framework. A custom
JRPG-inspired environment serves as the primary controlled benchmark for in-depth analysis, enabling pre -
cise measurement of phase-dependent assistance effects. To evaluate cross-domain robustness, complementary
experiments in ViZDoom (Appendix B) assess performance under different dynamics, observation structures,
and temporal regimes.
5.1 Environment setup
To evaluate the proposed mediated RL–LLM framework, we consider a custom turn-based battle environment
inspired by early JRPGs, particularly the first titles of the Final Fantasy series (Fig. 3). This class of environments
represents a challenging instance of sequential decision-making problems, combining discrete action choices,
stochastic opponent responses, and long-term resource management.
The environment models a simplified one-on-one combat scenario between an RL-controlled NPC and an
enemy with a fixed strategy. At each turn, the NPC acts first, followed by the enemy. The episode ends when
either HP reaches zero, with victory assigned to the NPC if the opponent falls first. This setting reduces unnec -
essary complexity while preserving the strategic trade-offs required to analyze adaptive decision-making and
phase-dependent assistance.

Environment characteristics. Both the NPC and the enemy are described by standard JRPG attributes,
including HP, magic points (MP), and attack and defense statistics. The action space includes offensive abilities,
healing actions, and consumable items, each characterized by different costs, effects, and availability constraints.

Reward and scoring overview. To support stable learning and expose risk-sensitive trade-offs, we adopt a

influences learning stability, exploration efficiency, and policy convergence.

5.2 Experimental setup
5.2.1 RL algorithm
The NPC is trained using the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm [ 55], which is well suited to discrete, turn-
based environments (Section 6). Moreover, DQN has demonstrated strong performance in managing complex,
high-dimensional environments, including Atari 2600 games [ 56, 57]. The DQN architecture used consists of
three hidden layers with widths of 128, 128, and 64 units respectively, each using ReLU activation functions.
The output layer is linear and has dimensionality equal to the number of discrete actions. This network depth
and width were selected to balance representational capacity and training stability in the considered turn-based
environment. Experience replay is implemented using a replay buffer of fixed size 2000 transitions, from which
mini-batches are uniformly sampled during training. This mechanism reduces correlation between consecutive
samples and improves data efficiency. A target network is employed to stabilize training. Its parameters are hard-
updated by copying the online network weights every 10 training steps, corresponding to a standard periodic
update strategy. The network is optimized using the Adam optimizer and mean squared error (MSE) loss.

agent. Specifically, Baseline RL serves as a reference point for evaluating the capabilities of the learning agent
alone. LLM-as-a-Judge enables the assessment of incorporating an LLM as an external evaluator of the actions
selected by the agent, providing the corresponding reward signal. Baseline Helper isolates the raw impact of

to incorporate LLM-based recommendations into the RL training process. For the last two settings, three variants
are considered:
● Initial Stage: The NPC relies on LLM recommendations only during the early phase of a match.
● Final Stage: The NPC incorporates LLM recommendations primarily toward the end of a match. Since the total
number of turns in a match cannot be predetermined, this variant is implemented probabilistically. Specifically,
we introduce a threshold parameter ϑ, initialized at 1. At each turn, a random value in [0, 1] is drawn: if the
value exceeds ϑ, the NPC adopts the LLM recommendation; otherwise, it follows the RL agent’s decision.
After each turn, ϑ is decreased by a fixed decrement κ, thereby increasing the likelihood of adopting LLM rec-
ommendations as the match progresses. We experiment with κ ∈{ 0.1, 0.2}, ensuring that recommendations
are more likely to be adopted in later turns.
● Random Stage: At each turn, the NPC randomly selects between the RL agent’s decision and the LLM recom-
mendation. This variant enables us to assess whether RL training benefits from sporadic and unsystematic
incorporation of LLM-driven generalization, as opposed to structured or stage-specific guidance.
5.2.4 Dataset construction for Reviewer training
To enable the two-phase training of the Reviewer, we constructed a labeled dataset specifically designed to
capture the interaction between game states, responses from Helper, and Reviewer feedback. The dataset
includes: (i) the current battle state, described in terms of the NPC’s HP and MP, the set of actions available at
that point in the game for the NPC, the enemy’s HP, and the last action performed by the enemy; (ii) a preliminary
response generated by the Helper; and ( iii) the Reviewer’s corrective instruction. A total of 15, 000 game
states were randomly generated to ensure wide coverage of possible combat scenarios. Variability was introduced
by systematically altering the NPC’s health and magic points, the enemy’s health, and the subset of actions made
available, thereby replicating both favorable and critical battle conditions. The preliminary responses associ -
ated with each state were obtained by reproducing patterns observed in the early experimental phases of the
Helper, including both strategically sound choices and suboptimal ones. Reviewer instructions were then
defined according to manually designed heuristic rules that reflect fundamental strategic principles. Specifically,
the heuristics favor actions that maximize damage against the opponent when conditions are advantageous, while
recommending healing whenever the NPC’s health drops below 30% of its maximum value. This design choice
ensures that the dataset encodes both offensive and defensive priorities, encouraging the Reviewer to balance
between exploiting powerful attacks and preserving survivability in critical conditions. The resulting dataset thus

5.2.5 Evaluation metrics
To evaluate performance, we adopt:
● Win rate: percentage of victories across episodes.
● Average reward: cumulative reward per match.
● Average moves: number of turns per match.
● Action score: measure of the quality of chosen actions given the current state and available resources.
This combined evaluation allows us to assess not only whether the NPC wins more frequently, but also whether it
develops strategies that are efficient, resource-aware, and robust across different battle contexts.

Model Configuration. For the training of the Reviewer, the dataset comprising 15, 000 instances was par-

with lighter configurations (e.g., reduced Reviewer frequency) further lowering costs.
Hardware, Latency, and Token Throughput. All experiments were conducted on a Mac Studio equipped
with the Apple M3 Ultra chip, featuring a high-performance multi-core CPU architecture, an integrated high-
throughput GPU, and large-bandwidth unified memory. Inference of open-weight models was performed locally
through LM Studio, leveraging Apple Silicon acceleration and the tightly integrated CPU-GPU-Neural Engine
architecture. The RL policy and environment simulation were executed locally, sharing the unified memory
resources.
The per-step latency is influenced by both the number of Helper invocations and the occurrence of Reviewer
interventions. In the average scenario, which involves a single Helper query, inference latency across all evalu-
ated LLMs is on the order of several hundred milliseconds. When Reviewer feedback necessitates an addi -
tional Helper invocation, the latency increases proportionally; however, it remains suitable for non-real-time
simulation contexts. The computational overhead introduced by the RL policy head and the environment logic is
negligible relative to the cost of language model generation.
Regarding token throughput, given the generation capabilities achievable on Apple Silicon hardware, the aver-
age across the models considered yields an effective processing capacity of approximately 20 tokens per second.
In real-time or compute-constrained scenarios, Reviewer frequency and maximum generation length can be
reduced to further optimize throughput and overall latency.

Turn-based analysis
Table 4 reports experiments evaluating the effect of varying the number of assisted turns within the best-
performing configuration, HeRoNInitial . Qwen 3 32B was selected as the Helper model, given its superior
performance in early-game assistance when paired with the Reviewer. Previous analyses indicated an average
match duration of 10.6 turns; therefore, assistance was limited to at most half of this length. When only one or two
turns were assisted, success rates remained low (7.5% and 9.2%), suggesting that such minimal guidance cannot
meaningfully influence decision-making and merely delays failure. Increasing support to three turns produced a
good improvement (25.1% success, 0.64 score), while four turns unexpectedly caused a decline (11% success).
The best performance occurred with five assisted turns, achieving 54.4% success and the highest action score
(0.70). This non-monotonic pattern suggests that four assisted turns represent a transitional regime: insufficient
for establishing a robust policy, yet excessive to foster autonomous adaptation. The agent may become partially
dependent on external input without receiving enough structured guidance to convert it into consistent strategies.
Such incomplete alignment likely explains the observed performance collapse. Testing further confirmed these
dynamics. Minimal assistance (one-two turns) yielded negligible generalization (7.6–15.6% success). Three
turns resulted in moderate performance (38%, 0.58 score), whereas four turns further reduced success (28.8%,
0.80 score). With five assisted turns, the agent achieved the best generalization: 81.2% success and 0.90 score,
confirming that this configuration provides the most effective balance between guided exploration and autono -
mous consolidation.
In summary, limited early guidance (three turns) supports initial learning but remains suboptimal for long-
term performance. Excessive intermediate guidance (four turns) disrupts adaptation, whereas five assisted turns
achieve optimal synergy between Helper recommendations and autonomous decision-making, yielding the
most robust and generalizable behavior.

Hallucination Analysis
To evaluate the influence of the Reviewer on Helper recommendations, we analyze hallucination scenarios
generated by LLMs during NPC training. In this work, we adopt an operational and environment-grounded defi-
nition of hallucination: an LLM output is considered hallucinated if it proposes an action that violates the explicit
constraints of the game environment or refers to entities, states, or effects that are not valid in the current context.
This definition enables automated and reproducible detection, avoiding subjective or post-hoc interpretation.
Automated hallucination detection. Hallucinations are detected through a deterministic, rule-based proce -
dure applied to each LLM-generated action before execution. An action is flagged as hallucinated if at least one of
the following conditions holds: (i) the action is not part of the environment’s admissible action set; (ii) the action
references non-existent entities, skills, or items; ( iii) required preconditions are not satisfied in the current state
(e.g., insufficient resources or invalid targets); (iv) the output does not conform to the expected structured action
format. This automated procedure is applied consistently across all configurations and models.
Error taxonomy. Detected hallucinations are further categorized into four error types: (A) Invalid Actions,
where the proposed action does not exist in the environment; (B) State-Inconsistent Actions, where the action is
valid in principle but incompatible with the current state; (C) Resource Hallucinations, involving references to
non-existent items; (D) Format Errors, where the output cannot be parsed into a valid action representation. This
taxonomy allows a finer-grained interpretation of hallucination behavior across LLMs.

Model-Specific Performance Analysis
Qwen consistently achieved the highest performance ( 81.2% success rate in HeRoNInitial ), which can be
attributed to its hybrid thinking mode framework enabling step-by-step reasoning for complex problem-solving.
The model’s post-training pipeline, which incorporates long chain-of-thought cold start and reasoning-based RL,
provides robust capabilities for sequential strategic inference. Crucially, Qwen exhibited minimal hallucinations
(only 1 instance in HeRoNInitial ), indicating strong grounding within environment-defined action constraints.
Similarly, Gemma demonstrated solid but more variable performance ( 75% success rate, ±14% variance vs.
Qwen’s ±4.7%). Its post-training approach combines knowledge distillation with RL fine-tuning using reward
functions designed to improve reasoning and instruction-following. However, its multimodal training focus may
introduce some instability when applied to the purely text-based, highly structured prompts of the JRPG envi -
ronment. Despite this variability, Gemma produced 0 hallucinations, confirming reliable adherence to the action
space. In contrast, Llama, despite being the largest model (70B parameters), achieved only moderate performance
(40.4% success rate). While Llama excels in general instruction-following benchmarks [ 61], these capabilities
did not translate to the strategic reasoning patterns required in turn-based combat. Its training emphasis on gen -
eral instruction-following and coding tasks may not align optimally with the domain-specific decision-making
required. Finally, Yi exhibited consistently poor performance across all HeRoN configurations, achieving a 0%
success rate in HeRoNInitial and only marginal improvements under alternative settings. These limitations likely
reflect Yi’s design priorities: as a bilingual English-Chinese foundation model, Yi relies on supervised fine-tun -
ing with fewer than 10 K curated instruction-response pairs using a next-token prediction objective. While this
approach may enhance general linguistic competence, it appears insufficient for constrained strategic reasoning
tasks. Hallucination analysis further highlights these deficiencies: despite employing the Reviewer, Yi gener-
ated 60 hallucinations in HeRoNInitial , in contrast to only 1 for Qwen and none for Gemma or Llama. These
hallucinations primarily consist of state-inconsistent actions and formatting errors, indicating a failure to ground
outputs within the environment-defined action space and to comply with the required output specifications.

7 Discussion
This section interprets the empirical findings from a broader RL perspective and discusses practical implications
for deploying mediated RL-LLM systems.
7.1 Phase-dependent guidance and policy learning dynamics
Experimental results show that the effectiveness of LLM-mediated assistance is phase-dependent: early guid -
ance improves exploration and generalization, whereas late intervention reduces stability and performance. This
asymmetry indicates that timing is a structural factor in policy learning rather than a mere implementation detail.
From an RL perspective, early assistance operates in a high-entropy regime where the policy remains explor -
atory and value estimates are poorly calibrated. In this phase, language guidance biases exploration toward stra-
tegically relevant actions without disrupting consolidated representations. When introduced later, however, the
policy is typically low-entropy and near-deterministic; external intervention perturbs stabilized Q-value struc -
tures, causing oscillations and miscalibrated updates. This effect can be viewed as non-stationary bias injection:
guidance that is beneficial during exploration becomes disruptive after convergence. These findings emphasize
the need for temporal control in hybrid RL-LLM systems, suggesting that assistance should be scheduled or
phased out rather than applied continuously.
Within the RL literature, these findings relate to curriculum learning [64] and policy shaping [65], but highlight
a complementary dimension: not progressive task modification or static action biasing, but the controlled intro -
duction and withdrawal of mediated guidance during training. In this view, HeRoN enables time-aware policy
shaping, where assistance is selectively integrated through a mediated pipeline rather than permanently imposed
on the policy. More broadly, the results suggest that hybrid architectures benefit from separating suggestion,
validation, and execution. This functional decomposition allows external reasoning modules to support learning
without overriding the control policy, preserving the stability of standard single-agent RL.
7.2 Practical, ethical, and deployment considerations
The mediated design of HeRoN has implications for deployment in commercial interactive environments. As
language components act as advisory modules rather than autonomous agents, they can be selectively enabled
during training, testing, or runtime based on performance, cost, or latency constraints.
The architecture is model-agnostic and compatible with both proprietary and open-weight LLMs. This flex -
ibility enables deployment configurations aligned with licensing terms, distribution requirements, and data gover-
nance policies. In particular, developers may choose locally hosted models for on-device inference, or API-based
services when scalability and maintenance considerations prevail.
From an ethical perspective, the framework operates at the decision-support level for NPC behavior rather
than generating player-facing narrative content [ 34–37]. This reduces exposure to biased or inappropriate lan -
guage outputs and preserves designer control over high-level gameplay dynamics. Furthermore, the presence
of a Reviewer module introduces an explicit validation layer, mitigating risks associated with hallucinated or
strategically inconsistent recommendations.
Finally, practical deployment must consider inference latency and token cost. While LLM-mediated guidance
can improve training efficiency, real-time integration may require selective activation strategies or lightweight
model choices. The mediated structure of HeRoN supports such configurations, enabling adaptive trade-offs
between computational overhead and behavioral sophistication.

8 Conclusion and future work
This work introduced HeRoN, a mediated RL-LLM framework to enhance NPC decision-making in interactive
environments. The architecture centralizes learning within a single RL-driven NPC policy, while LLMs act as
auxiliary reasoning modules providing structured, context-aware guidance. HeRoN comprises three components:
an RL-controlled NPC executing actions, a zero-shot Helper generating strategy proposals with rationales, and
a lightweight Reviewer ensuring coherence with game constraints. Experimental results in a JRPG-inspired
turn-based setting and a first-person shooting environment show that selective language mediation effectively
integrates LLMs into NPC design. Among the configurations, HeRoNInitial proved to be the most effective,
as it supports guided exploration and coherent policy formation, after which the agent consolidates behavior
autonomously. These findings underscore the importance of calibrated mediation when coupling RL and LLMs,
particularly where hallucinations or constraint violations affect gameplay quality.
Future work will extend HeRoN to additional genres, including open-world and platform games with faster
and less predictable dynamics. Further directions include evaluation in higher-complexity 2D and 3D environ -
ments, multi-opponent scenarios, and collaborative settings where multiple NPCs coordinate under centralized
learning.

higher thresholds (35-40%) induce overly conservative behavior, prematurely prioritizing defense at the expense
of offensive progress, whereas lower thresholds (around 20%) position the agent too close to defeat, substantially
increasing the probability of immediate failure. The selected value therefore represents a balanced compromise
between robustness and strategic aggressiveness.

and combat in order to successfully complete the task. The reward function is distance-based and goal-oriented.
At each timestep, the agent receives a positive reward +dX when reducing its distance to the vest, and a negative
reward −dX when moving farther away from it, where dX represents the magnitude of the distance variation.

B.2. Experimental setup
B. 2. 1 Model configuration
Across all experiments, the Helper relies on the Qwen 3 32B model, selected as the most effective model in
prior evaluations in the JRPG domain. Consistently, DQN was employed for the implementation of the NPC,
given its superior performance in the same evaluation setting.
The Helper Prompt (Fig. 10) encodes key state information (HP, available ammo, available actions, and the

a structured representation of the current state information and the initial action recommendation generated by
the Helper (Fig. 11). When a refinement is proposed, the Helper is re-prompted using the Helper-Reviewer
Prompt (Fig. 12).
To illustrate, consider a scenario in which the agent is exposed to multiple shooting monsters and the Helper

instead suggest a safer alternative, such as repositioning (e.g., moving laterally or turning to face a nearby threat)
before proceeding forward, in order to reduce incoming damage and avoid premature death. Conversely, if the
Helper proposes excessive evasive movements that stall progress, the Reviewer can highlight the need to
resume forward navigation to maintain positive distance-based rewards.
Through this mechanism, the Reviewer acts as a strategic consistency layer, balancing goal-directed naviga-
tion with survival constraints.

the set of available discrete actions; (ii) a preliminary response generated by the Helper; and (iii) the Reviewer’s

The root node is a priority selector with three main branches:
● survivability management;
● resource management;
● offensive strategy.The evaluation proceeds from left to right, meaning that higher-priority branches preempt
the execution of lower-priority ones as soon as their conditions are satisfied.
The survivability branch is structured as a sequence node. It first evaluates whether the current HP of the NPC
falls below a predefined healing threshold. If the condition is met, the node checks the availability of healing
actions (e.g., sufficient MP or consumable items). If executable, the corresponding healing action is triggered
immediately. This ensures that defensive recovery strictly dominates any offensive behavior when the agent is at
risk.
The second branch handles resource management. It activates when MP falls below the minimum cost required
to execute any skill-based offensive action. Similar to the survival branch, it first evaluates a condition node (MP
availability) and then selects an MP recovery action if feasible. This design prevents deadlock states where the
NPC would otherwise repeatedly attempt infeasible magic actions.
The final branch encodes the offensive policy. It consists of a selector node that ranks available attack options
according to predefined damage heuristics. Physical attacks and spells are ordered deterministically based on
their expected effective damage, considering MP feasibility constraints. The highest-priority executable offensive
action is selected. If no magic action can be performed due to insufficient MP, the tree falls back to the default
physical attack.
All thresholds (e.g., HP healing trigger, MP sufficiency) are fixed constants defined a priori and calibrated
manually. Importantly, the BT contains no adaptive mechanism: no parameter updates, no state-dependent learn-
ing, and no stochastic exploration. Its behavior is fully deterministic, meaning that identical game states always
yield identical action selections.
The BT was implemented programmatically using modular node classes (Selector, Sequence, Condition,
Action), allowing explicit control over execution order and termination logic.
D. LLM-as-a-judge implementation
This appendix presents the implementation used for the LLM-as-a-judge approach. It is designed to provide
an external evaluative signal for the action selected by the RL-controlled NPC. Rather than directly proposing
actions, the model receives as input the current game state and the action chosen by the agent, and outputs a scalar
quality assessment in the range [1, 10] (following [44, 45]), representing the estimated effectiveness of that action
in the given context.
The evaluation prompt follows a structured template with two main components:
● a formal description of the game state and available actions;
● the action selected by the agent at that specific decision step.The prompt explicitly instructs the LLM to assess
the action according to tactical effectiveness, resource efficiency, and survivability impact, and to output only
a numerical score between 1 and 10 (Fig. 13). This constrained output format ensures direct compatibility with
the reward pipeline and reduces ambiguity in parsing.
To ensure stability of the reward signal, inference is performed with temperature set to 0. This configuration
eliminates stochastic variation in outputs, guaranteeing deterministic scoring for identical state-action pairs.

The raw LLM score s ∈ [1, 10] is linearly normalized to the interval [−1, 1]. The normalization ensures sym-
metric bounds, enabling both positive reinforcement for high-quality actions and negative feedback for poor
choices [44].
At each decision step, the RL agent selects an action according to its current policy. The environment transition
is executed, and the resulting state-action pair is passed to the LLM. The normalized score replaces the environ-
ment reward signal and is used to update the policy parameters.
E. Helper-Reviewer interactions during match
This Appendix presents full-match qualitative examples illustrating the complete Helper-Reviewer-
NPC decision pipeline. Specifically, we report full interaction transcripts for HeRoNInitial , HeRoNF inal, and
HeRoNRandom (Tables 21-24), enabling a qualitative comparison of how temporally conditioned mediated guid-
ance affects strategic evolution throughout an episode. For each interaction step, the game state immediately
preceding the decision, the Helper’s recommendation, the Reviewer’s critique, and the final action executed
by the NPC are reported, together with the resulting game state after the interaction.
The examples highlight clear behavioral differences among the three configurations. In HeRoNInitial , guid-
ance during early turns consistently promotes the selection of the highest-impact offensive action (Table 21). In
the first three mediated turns, the Helper repeatedly proposes blizzard spell (800 damage, 35 MP), a
reasonable high-damage option. However, the Reviewer consistently upgrades this choice to meteor spell
(1000 damage, 40 MP). Although the difference in MP cost is marginal (5 MP), the damage increase of 200
HP per turn compounds significantly across consecutive turns. After three optimized actions, the enemy HP is
reduced from 5000 to 2980 instead of remaining above 3400 under Blizzard usage. This acceleration reduces total
combat duration and, crucially, limits the number of enemy retaliation turns. In a later mediated turn the Helper
proposes the use of potion. From a superficial survival perspective, this suggestion reflects cautious reasoning:
MP is nearly depleted and conserving magical resources might appear prudent. However, the quantitative impact
of potion is minimal (50 HP), especially when the NPC is not in immediate lethal range. The marginal recovery
does not meaningfully alter survivability nor accelerate enemy HP reduction.

that reshape the entire match trajectory, transforming a potentially balanced encounter into a controlled progres-

In contrast, HeRoNF inal illustrates the limitations of late-stage intervention (Table 22). In the first state, the
NPC executes cura Spell (1500 HP heal at 32 MP). Since the NPC is already near maximum HP, the healing
provides negligible marginal benefit while consuming substantial MP. Shortly afterward, elixir is used at 2738
HP and 100 MP, again without critical necessity. These early resource expenditures represent strategic misalloca-
tions: high-value recovery tools are consumed in non-critical conditions. When mediation becomes active (e.g., at
2040 HP and 102 MP), the Reviewer successfully refines the Helper’s offensive choices–promoting meteor
over blizzard, increasing per-turn damage from 800 to 1000. Locally, this correction mirrors the behavior
observed in HeRoNInitial . However, the broader trajectory differs. By this stage, the NPC’s HP buffer is already
reduced, and Elixir is no longer available for a late reset. The match ultimately ends with the NPC at 0 HP while
the enemy retains substantial health. The qualitative trace makes clear that late optimization cannot compensate
for structurally flawed early decisions. Unlike in HeRoNInitial , mediation improves local action quality but fails
to alter the match’s global outcome due to accumulated resource inefficiencies.
HeRoNRandom reveals two distinct full-match behaviors. In Table 23, early turns proceed without mediation
(e.g., Attack, Fire, Grenade). Mediation activates later at 2133 HP and 45 MP against 3203 HP. Here, the Helper
proposes blizzard, and the Reviewer upgrades it to meteor, increasing damage output precisely at a
moment when MP reserves are sufficient for high-impact casting but not abundant. Later, when the NPC drops
to 561 HP and only 5 MP, the Helper correctly proposes elixir, and the Reviewer confirms. The timing
is critical: both HP and MP are nearly exhausted, and the enemy still has 1410 HP. In this configuration, Elixir
enables full recovery and renews offensive potential, creating a second decisive phase that leads to victory.
In Table 24, early mediation occurs at the initial state. In this opening configuration, the Helper correctly
proposes meteor spell, leveraging the full MP pool to maximize immediate damage output (1000 HP for 40
MP). The Reviewer validates this choice, recognizing it as strictly dominant in a high-resource, low-risk state.
However, as the match progresses, mediation becomes intermittent. The Helper, when the NPC has 2209 HP,
52 MP against 2994 HP, selects blizzard and is corrected by Reviewer who recommends meteor, while
in the next turn the Helper’s choice is confirmed (grenade). From the next turn, Helper and Reviewer no
longer intervene, and the NPC chooses actions that lead to defeat ( potion, grenade, potion) This duality
illustrates both the strength and the variability of randomized mediation. Although it does not guaranty consistent
optimal trajectories, it preserves the capacity to intervene adaptively at decisive states and occasionally overturn
adverse dynamics. Together, these qualitative analyses complement the quantitative findings, clarifying how the
temporal positioning of structured linguistic guidance fundamentally shapes strategic consistency, recoverability,
and match-level outcomes.
