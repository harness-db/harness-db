Description

The multi-agent systems community spent two decades formalizing organizations — roles, groups, norms, reorganization — for agents that could barely act. The LLM era inverted the problem: agents are suddenly capable, and the organizational layer is missing. We report on what is, to our knowledge, among the first documented production implementations of the organization-centered multi-agent systems (OCMAS) formalism on an LLM substrate: a business operating system running nine tenant organizations on one codebase, with roles as data, a regimented deontic layer, a blackboard coordination environment, and a falsifiable decision ledger. The paper's central contribution is a negative result and its cure. Three successive audits of the running system found (1) a fully governed organization that sensed, deliberated, decided, and logged while delivering nothing — a failure mode we name performed autonomy; (2) a subtler variant in which real delivery resumed while the governance around it was performed; and (3) a documented revival, verified by an end-to-end trace re-derivable by ID. We formalize performed autonomy, present the substrate-level mechanisms that make it structurally impossible (outcome-keyed completion, duty-roster coverage, falsifiability as a commit gate), and distill three design laws for governed LLM organizations.
